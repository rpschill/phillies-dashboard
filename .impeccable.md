# Phillies Dashboard — Design Context

## Design Context

### Users

**Primary:** Ryan Schill — personal daily use, primarily pre-game (checking schedule, next game, probable pitchers). Also used during and after games for scores and stats.

**Secondary:** Hiring managers, recruiters, and potential clients viewing the project as a portfolio piece. The dashboard must demonstrate design taste and frontend craft alongside technical skill.

Design decisions should serve the daily user first, but the overall polish and cohesion should hold up to professional scrutiny.

### Brand Personality

**Three words:** Warm. Bold. Readable.

**Emotional goal:** The feeling of a summer afternoon heading to Citizens Bank Park — anticipation, sunshine, the smell of ballpark food, red and white against a blue sky. Not the tension of a playoff scoreboard or the sterility of a stats spreadsheet.

**Voice:** Confident and direct, never cluttered or shouty. Information-dense but never overwhelming.

### Aesthetic Direction

**Visual tone:** Stadium energy channeled through warmth and clarity. Bold use of Phillies Red and Navy as anchors, with warm off-white and natural tones carrying the background. Not clinical. Not grid-heavy.

**Light mode:** Warm off-white (`#f7f6f3`) backgrounds, white cards, natural shadows. Feels like paper scorecards and sunshine.

**Dark mode:** Deep navy backgrounds (not pure black). Navy + Red on dark feels like stadium lights at night — electric, not harsh.

**Anti-references:** ESPN, The Athletic, Baseball Reference, and generic sports sites. The shared aesthetic of those sites — white backgrounds, black grid lines dividing data, dense blue hyperlinks — should be actively avoided. This dashboard should feel like nothing else in the sports category.

**Positive references:** The feeling (not the look) of a well-designed weather app — glanceable, beautiful, purposeful. Also: ballpark scoreboards, vintage baseball card typography, the warmth of a summer day.

### Design Principles

1. **Readability above all else.** Information hierarchy is non-negotiable. No aesthetic choice should make the data harder to read. Score, game state, and next game should be legible at a glance.

2. **Warmth over sterility.** Prefer warm neutrals, organic shapes, and soft shadows over sharp grids and clinical white. The palette should evoke the feeling of being at the ballpark, not reading a spreadsheet.

3. **Bold accents, calm foundations.** Phillies Red and Navy are expressive colors — use them with intention as focal points. The backgrounds and typography carry the load; color punctuates it.

4. **Distinctive by design.** Every component should be a conscious departure from the ESPN/Athletic aesthetic. White backgrounds with black dividers = default; avoid by default. Seek warmth, roundness, texture, and character instead.

5. **Portfolio-grade polish.** This is both a daily tool and a public showcase. Every screen should demonstrate that design decisions were intentional — spacing, hierarchy, motion, and accessibility should all show craft.

### Technical Constraints & Accessibility

- **WCAG 2.1 AA** compliance required. All text/background color pairs must meet contrast ratios (4.5:1 for normal text, 3:1 for large text).
- **Both light and dark mode** supported via CSS custom properties / Tailwind dark variant.
- Consider reduced motion for animations (live indicator pulse, any transitions).
- Color blindness: don't rely solely on red/green to indicate win/loss — pair with icons or text labels.

### Stack

- Angular 19.2 + Tailwind CSS 4.2
- SCSS modules per component with shared tokens in `src/styles/_variables.scss`
- Tailwind theme tokens via `@theme` directive in `src/styles.scss`
- Inter font (Google Fonts, variable weight 100–900)
- Brand colors: Red `#E81828`, Navy `#002D72`
